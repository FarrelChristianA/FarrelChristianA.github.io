# Why did the ECB's hike barely move the euro?

**Case study, week of 5 to 19 September 2026. Public data only.**

Page: [`projects/ecb-week.html`](../ecb-week.html)

On Thursday 10 September 2026 the European Central Bank raised its deposit rate by 25 basis points to 2.50%. EUR/USD barely reacted. This case follows that week the way a research desk would, with each document written as if on its own date.

## The short answer

- **Priced in.** On 2 September the Bundesbank President said markets priced a more than 95% probability of a September hike.
- **No new signal.** The decision was unanimous and the guidance sentence did not change. The projections leaned hawkish, yet US producer prices landed fifteen minutes later.
- **A small reaction.** The ECB reference rate went from 1.1616 (10 Sep) to 1.1592 (11 Sep), a fall of 0.21%.
- **Normal, not special.** Across eight ECB meetings, the average move on the following day was 0.64 times a normal day. One decision day in eight passed a 1.5 flag, against 20.6% of all days.

## Method and limits

- **Data:** ECB euro reference rates, CFTC Traders in Financial Futures, Eurostat, BLS, US Treasury, FRED, ECB press releases and accounts, Bundesbank.
- **Event study:** change on the following day in EUR/USD after each decision, divided by the average absolute daily change over the prior 60 fixings.
- **Limits:** one fixing a day hides intraday moves; the following day window mixes in other news; eight meetings is a small sample.
