01 Telkom University

Learning to ask where it breaks

I studied Industrial Engineering in Bandung. The course trained two questions into me. Where can this process fail, and how do you stop it failing the same way twice? And how do you make the work easier and faster without breaking it? Statistics, operations research and production systems were the tools. I finished with a 3.74 GPA, and the habit stayed.

02 Lab, internship and a capstone

Teaching, then building

For more than two years I taught CAD as a laboratory assistant. I wrote 16 modules, trained more than 100 students and kept over 20 sessions a week running. In 2024 I did my internship at PT Dirgantara Indonesia on the NC212i aircraft program, tracking production data in Excel and finding what slowed final assembly down.

The same year I led a team of three that built a working electric scooter: 45 kph, 21 km of range, and the Best Capstone Project award. Since then I have also helped run my family's stationery shop: stock, customers, cash and keeping the machines running.

03 Market research

Into the markets

It started in July 2025 with crypto. I didn't go all in. I went back to the basics instead: books, video lectures, and when that wasn't enough, a six month course that taught me macroeconomics and how central banks move markets. I learned risk before size.

A year later that study turned into a job. In July 2026 I started as a Macro Analyst, writing research for professional traders. Every day means futures positioning, economic releases and central bank decisions. In this work a wrong number costs trust, so every figure gets checked against its source and every correction becomes a written rule.

To show how that work fits together, I rebuilt one real week from public data: a central bank decision followed from the Saturday before to the Saturday after. Read the case study.

A wrong number costs more than a late one. So I check first, and I write down what I learn.
04 AI, from zero

Learning to build with AI

In early 2026, when my work at the shop got lighter, I started learning AI properly. I went from plain chat prompts to AI agents, and now I use them every day to make work faster. I have no coding background, so I learned to build the way an engineer would: write the spec, set the rules, test every change.

That is how my tools came to exist: a trading dashboard, an AI job screener that ran my own job hunt, a macro learning workspace, a finance app and a productivity dashboard. At work I set up the AI side of my research the same way: memory files, rules, checklists and a fact log. Next, I want to bring that same habit to a team: make the work easier, and keep it right.
