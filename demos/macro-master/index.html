Macro Framework Overview
The big picture — why macro matters and how all pieces connect
What is Macro Trading? Macro trading is the discipline of analyzing the global economic environment — monetary policy, inflation, growth, capital flows, and geopolitics — to determine the direction and momentum of asset prices across all classes: equities, bonds, FX, commodities, and crypto. Unlike micro analysis (stock picking), macro tells you which environment you're in and which assets benefit.
🌍 The Macro Universe
Macro operates at the intersection of four forces:

1. Monetary Policy — Central banks set the price of money (interest rates). Fed, PBoC, BI, ECB, BOJ all matter.

2. Fiscal Policy — Government spending and taxation. Deficits expand liquidity. Surpluses contract it.

3. Growth & Inflation — The two variables every central bank juggles. Strong growth + high inflation = hike rates. Weak growth + low inflation = cut rates.

4. Capital Flows — Money chases yield and safety globally. When US yields rise, capital flows into USD. When they fall, capital flows into EM or risk assets.

🌊 Global Liquidity Cycle (Michael Howell Framework)

CrossBorder Capital's Global Liquidity Index tracks money flowing through ~90 countries. The cycle runs ~65 months peak-to-peak.

Federal Reserve & Monetary Policy
The most powerful macro force in global markets — every trader must understand this
🏛️ What is the Federal Reserve?
The Fed is the US central bank. Its dual mandate: (1) Maximum employment and (2) Price stability (target 2% inflation). Every policy decision is a tradeoff between these two goals.

FOMC (Federal Open Market Committee) — the 12-member body that votes on interest rates. Meets 8 times per year. Their decisions move ALL global markets within seconds.

📈 Interest Rate Policy (FFR) ▼
The Fed Funds Rate is the primary tool. Raising rates makes borrowing expensive → slows economy → reduces inflation. Cutting rates stimulates borrowing → grows economy → risks inflation.

Key rule: 2Y Treasury yield ≈ market's expectation of where FFR will be in 2 years. If 2Y yield > current FFR → market expects hikes. If 2Y yield < FFR → market expects cuts. Watch this spread daily.
💰 Quantitative Easing (QE) ▼
QE = Fed buys bonds (Treasuries, MBS) from the market. This:
1. Adds reserves to banking system (money supply expands)
2. Pushes bond prices UP → yields DOWN
3. Forces investors into riskier assets (stocks, crypto) seeking returns
4. Weakens the dollar over time

Effect: Risk assets rally hard during QE periods. See 2020-2021 — $4T+ QE → Bitcoin went from $4k to $69k.
🔥 Quantitative Tightening (QT) ▼
QT = Fed lets bonds mature without reinvesting (or sells bonds). This:
1. Removes reserves from banking system (money supply contracts)
2. Bond prices fall → yields RISE
3. Investors shift from risky to safer assets
4. Strengthens the dollar

Effect: Headwind for all risk assets. QT was a key driver of the 2022 bear market in both equities and crypto.
📋 Forward Guidance & Dot Plot ▼
Dot Plot: Issued quarterly, shows each FOMC member's projection for future Fed Funds Rate. Markets move significantly when the dot plot is more hawkish or dovish than expected.

Forward guidance is verbal communication of future intentions. "We will keep rates elevated until inflation sustainably returns to 2%" = hawkish guidance that caps rallies.

Where to find: CME FedWatch Tool at cmegroup.com — shows real-time probability of each rate outcome at each FOMC meeting.
🎯 How to Read an FOMC Statement — The Checklist
  • Rate Decision: Hike, cut, or hold? Compare to market expectations. A "hold" expected becomes hawkish if the statement language is aggressive.
  • Vote Count: 11-1 vs 7-5 vote = very different signals. A dissenting vote toward a hike = hawkish surprise.
  • Balance Sheet: Any changes to QT pace? This is as important as rates for liquidity.
  • Key Language Changes: Compare word-for-word to prior statement. "Modest" vs "moderate" economic activity can move markets.
  • Press Conference: Powell's tone matters. Real-time reaction. Look for: acknowledgment of risks, flexibility language, conditions for pivoting.
  • Summary of Economic Projections (SEP): Quarterly only. GDP, unemployment, inflation, and dot plot forecasts. Upward revision to inflation = hawkish.

Inflation Data (CPI / PCE / PPI)
The #1 driver of Fed policy and the most market-moving data in the world
Why Inflation Is Everything: Higher inflation → Fed hikes → yields rise → DXY strengthens → equities fall → crypto dumps. Lower inflation → Fed cuts → yields fall → DXY weakens → risk assets rally. Understanding inflation IS understanding the market cycle.
📊 Consumer Price Index (CPI)
Measures average change in prices paid by urban consumers for a basket of goods. Released ~2nd week of each month at 8:30 AM ET by BLS.

Key Numbers to Watch:
• Headline CPI YoY — total inflation including food and energy
• Core CPI YoY — excludes food & energy (more stable, Fed focuses here)
• CPI MoM — month-over-month change (0.2-0.3% = normal)

TradingView: FRED:CPIAUCSL ECONOMICS:USIRYY

📌 PCE — Personal Consumption Expenditures (The Fed's REAL Target)
PCE is the Fed's officially preferred inflation gauge, unlike CPI which is more media-followed. Released last Friday of each month at 8:30 AM ET by BEA.

Why PCE differs from CPI:
• Chained index — accounts for consumer substitution (if beef is expensive, people buy chicken)
• Broader basket — includes employer-paid healthcare, broader services
• Lower shelter weight — PCE gives less weight to owner-equivalent rent

PCE typically runs 0.3-0.5% lower than CPI. The Fed's 2% target is PCE, not CPI.

TradingView: FRED:PCEPI | Core PCE: FRED:PCEPILFE
🏭 PPI — Producer Price Index (Leading Indicator for CPI)
PPI measures price changes from the seller's (producer's) perspective. Released ~1-2 days before CPI each month.

Why PPI Matters as a Leading Indicator: Price increases at the producer level eventually pass through to consumers. Rising PPI → Rising CPI in 2-6 months. Traders use PPI to front-run CPI surprises.

Key sub-components:
• Final Demand PPI — goods and services sold to end users
• Core PPI — excludes food and energy
• Intermediate Demand — materials in production pipeline (ultra-early signal)

TradingView: FRED:PPIACO
📐 Break-Even Inflation (BEI) & Real Yields
BEI = Nominal Treasury yield minus TIPS (Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities) yield. It represents the market's expected average inflation over the bond's life.
BEI (5Y) = US05Y Nominal − FRED:DFII5 (5Y TIPS Real Yield)
Real Yield = Nominal Yield - Expected Inflation (BEI)

Why Real Yields Are Critical:
• Real yields rising = true tightening. Gold falls. DXY rises. Expensive for growth companies.
• Real yields falling = easing effect. Gold rallies hard. Growth stocks re-rate up.
• Negative real yields = best environment for gold and risk assets.

TradingView: 5Y Real Yield: FRED:DFII5 | 10Y Real Yield: FRED:DFII10

Labor Market — NFP, JOLTS, Unemployment
Employment is the Fed's second mandate — and the last to break in a downturn
Why Labor Matters: Strong jobs = consumers spend = inflation stays up = Fed stays hawkish. Weak jobs = Fed pivots dovish = rate cuts incoming = bonds and risk assets rally. Labor is a lagging indicator — by the time jobs break, recession is usually already here.
📊 Non-Farm Payrolls (NFP) — The King of Data
Released 1st Friday of every month, 8:30 AM ET. Measures jobs added/lost across the US economy (ex-farm sector).

Key components to read:
• Headline number — jobs added vs forecast (e.g., +200k vs +180k expected)
• Prior month revision — often more important than headline! Big downward revisions = weakness was worse
• Unemployment Rate (U-3) — % of labor force jobless and actively seeking
• Average Hourly Earnings (AHE) — wage inflation proxy. AHE hot = sticky inflation = hawkish
• Participation Rate — % of working-age population in labor force

📋 JOLTS — Job Openings & Labor Turnover
Released ~2 days before NFP. Key metric: Job Openings (demand for labor) vs Hires and Quits.

Quits Rate = "confidence indicator." People quit when confident they'll find another job. Falling quits = labor market softening = dovish signal.

Openings/Unemployed ratio: Above 1.0 = tight market (hawkish). Below 1.0 = loosening (dovish).

TV: FRED:JTSJOL
📉 Jobless Claims (Weekly)
Released every Thursday 8:30 AM ET. Two numbers:

• Initial Claims — new unemployment filings (weekly pulse). Rising = layoffs accelerating.
• Continuing Claims — people staying on unemployment. Rising = harder to find jobs.

Threshold: Initial Claims above 300k+ = recession risk. Below 200k = tight market.

TV: FRED:ICSA FRED:CCSA
📈 ADP Employment (NFP Preview)
Released Wednesday before NFP Friday. Private-sector payroll estimate from ADP. Not perfectly correlated to NFP but gives directional hint.

Correlation: ~50-60% directional accuracy with NFP. Use as setup, not prediction.

Also watch: ISM Employment Sub-index inside ISM Manufacturing/Services PMI reports — released before NFP and often a good lead.

🏗️ GDP — Gross Domestic Product
Total value of all goods and services produced. Released quarterly in 3 stages:

Advance Estimate (most important, ~30 days after quarter ends) → 2nd Estimate (~60 days) → 3rd/Final (~90 days)

Components (C + I + G + NX):
• C = Consumer spending (~70% of US GDP) — watch Retail Sales as proxy
• I = Business investment — capex, housing
• G = Government spending
• NX = Net exports (Trade Balance)

Two consecutive negative quarters = Technical Recession

Released ~mid-month. Measures total receipts at retail establishments. Consumer spending = 70% of GDP, so this is critical.

Bonds Deep Dive
The most important asset class — bonds lead everything else. Understand this first.
The Golden Rule of Bonds: Bond prices and yields move in OPPOSITE directions. When everyone buys bonds (flight to safety), prices go UP, yields go DOWN. When people sell bonds, prices go DOWN, yields go UP. If you remember nothing else about bonds, remember this.
📚 What Is a Bond?
A bond is a loan from an investor to a borrower (government or corporation). The borrower promises to:

1. Pay a fixed coupon (interest) periodically
2. Return the face value (par) at maturity

Example: You buy a $1,000 US Treasury bond with 5% coupon, 10-year maturity. You receive $50/year for 10 years, then get your $1,000 back.

Yield = annual return relative to current price.
If bond price falls to $900 → same $50 coupon → yield = 50/900 = 5.56% (higher yield)
If bond price rises to $1,100 → yield = 50/1100 = 4.55% (lower yield)

Yield Curve & TIPS
The most reliable recession indicator in history — and real yields as gold's compass
📐 What is the Yield Curve?
The yield curve plots Treasury yields across all maturities (3M → 30Y). Its shape tells you what the market expects for growth and rates.

ShapeLookSignal

Historical track record: Every US recession since 1960 was preceded by an inverted 2s10s curve. However, the recession typically arrives 12-24 months AFTER the inversion, and often only after the curve re-steepens (uninverts).
💡 TIPS & Real Yields — Gold's True Driver
TIPS = Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities. Principal adjusts with CPI. The yield on TIPS = real yield (return after inflation).

Real Yield = Nominal Yield − Break-Even Inflation (BEI)
Why this drives gold:
Gold pays no interest. When real yields are positive and rising, holding gold has an opportunity cost — you could earn real return from TIPS instead. So gold falls.

When real yields are negative or falling, gold has no competition → gold rallies.

Credit Spreads
The bond market's early warning system — credit cracks before equities do
Credit Spreads Lead Equity Markets: When credit spreads widen, it means lenders are demanding more compensation for risk — corporations are struggling to borrow cheaply. This typically shows up in the bond market BEFORE equities break down. Pro traders watch spreads first.
📏 What Are Credit Spreads?
A credit spread = yield on corporate bond MINUS yield on equivalent Treasury bond. It represents the extra compensation investors demand for taking on credit (default) risk.

Credit Spread = Corporate Bond Yield − Treasury Yield (same maturity)
Types:
• Investment Grade (IG) — bonds rated BBB- or above (low default risk). Spread ~100-200 bps normal.
• High Yield (HY) / Junk — bonds rated below BBB- (higher default risk). Spread ~300-500 bps normal.
• OAS (Option-Adjusted Spread) — spread adjusted for embedded options. The cleanest comparison metric.

Fed Balance Sheet & Liquidity
QE vs QT — the single biggest driver of all risk asset prices over the long run
🏦 The Federal Reserve Balance Sheet
The Fed's balance sheet (total assets) shows how much money the Fed has injected into the financial system. Released every Wednesday at 4:30 PM ET.

QE (Quantitative Easing): Fed BUYS bonds → pays banks with newly created reserves → balance sheet grows → money supply expands → risk assets rally.

QT (Quantitative Tightening): Fed lets bonds mature without reinvesting (or sells) → reserves shrink → money supply contracts → liquidity falls → headwind for risk assets.

TV: FRED:WALCL (updated weekly)

Historical context: 2008: ~$0.9T → 2022 peak: ~$9T → ~$6.6T by late 2025, when QT was wound down. Figures as of 2025 — check FRED:WALCL for the current level.
📊 M2 Money Supply
M2 = total money in circulation (cash + checking + savings + money market funds). The broadest widely-tracked measure of money supply.

M2 and Asset Prices: Strong correlation with stock and crypto prices. When M2 grows rapidly → more money chasing assets → prices rise. When M2 contracts → rare, but extremely bearish for risk assets (2022 was first M2 decline since 1929).

M2 Growth Rate = Year-over-year change. Above +8% = very accommodative. Below 0% = extreme tightening.

Net Liquidity — TGA & RRP Mechanics
The formula that explains 80% of crypto and risk asset moves
This is the most important formula for crypto traders. Net Liquidity explains why Bitcoin and the Nasdaq move in near-lockstep with Fed balance sheet changes. Every serious macro crypto trader tracks this weekly.
🧮 The Net Liquidity Formula

🏦 TGA — Treasury General Account
The US Treasury's checking account at the Federal Reserve. When Treasury collects taxes or sells bonds, cash flows INTO the TGA, which drains reserves from the banking system.

TGA Rising → Treasury hoarding cash → less money in banking system → liquidity contracts → risk assets face headwind

TGA Falling → Treasury spending its cash → money flows into banking system → liquidity expands → risk assets rally

Key event: Debt ceiling resolution → Treasury refills TGA rapidly by issuing T-bills → this can drain $300-500B in liquidity within weeks → historically bearish for crypto.

TV: FRED:WTREGEN
🏦 RRP — Overnight Reverse Repo
Money market funds (MMFs) have too much cash and no place to put it. They park it overnight at the Fed via RRP, earning the RRP rate. This cash is out of the financial system.

RRP Rising → More cash parked at Fed → less available for risk assets → bearish

RRP Falling → MMFs moving cash OUT of Fed and into T-bills or other assets → liquidity re-enters system → bullish for risk assets

2021-2022: RRP exploded from $0 to $2.5 trillion as QE flooded system with too much cash. From 2023, it drained back toward zero as T-bill rates became attractive. This RRP drain was a hidden liquidity tailwind for markets.

TV: FRED:RRPONTSYD

📦 What is an ETF? — Start From Zero
An ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund) is a financial product that trades on a traditional stock exchange (like NYSE or Nasdaq) and tracks the price of an underlying asset — in this case, Bitcoin or Ethereum.

Think of it like this: You want to own Bitcoin, but you don't want to deal with wallets, seed phrases, custody, or crypto exchanges. You can instead buy IBIT (BlackRock's Bitcoin ETF) through your regular brokerage — the same way you'd buy Apple stock. BlackRock holds actual BTC in custody on your behalf, and the share price moves exactly with BTC price.

Why this is huge for macro: Pension funds, hedge funds, retirement accounts (IRA/401k), and wealth managers were previously unable to hold Bitcoin due to regulatory restrictions. The ETF wrapper solves this. Trillions of dollars in traditional finance now have a direct, regulated path into BTC.
✅ Spot ETF vs ❌ Futures ETF
Spot ETF (IBIT, FBTC etc):
Holds actual Bitcoin. When you buy a share, the issuer buys real BTC. Price tracks spot 1:1. No decay, no roll cost. This is what was approved in January 2024 and changed everything.

Futures ETF (old BITO etc):
Holds BTC futures contracts, not real BTC. Every month, it must "roll" expiring contracts to the next month. In a contango market (futures > spot), rolling is expensive — the ETF constantly bleeds value. BITO lost ~30% vs BTC in its first year purely from roll costs. Avoid futures ETFs for long-term holding.

🏛️ What Are CME Futures? — Full Explanation
CME (Chicago Mercantile Exchange) is the world's largest regulated derivatives exchange. CME Bitcoin futures allow institutions to get leveraged exposure to BTC price in a regulated, familiar environment — no crypto wallet, no private keys, no counterparty risk from crypto exchanges.

Key mechanics:
• Contract size: 1 standard CME BTC contract = 5 BTC. At $100k BTC, that's $500,000 per contract.
• Settlement: Cash-settled in USD — no actual BTC changes hands. Price references CME CF Bitcoin Reference Rate (BRR).
• Margin: Need ~$50–100k initial margin to control $500k contract (~10x leverage max for regulated entities).
• Hours: CME trades Sunday 6pm – Friday 5pm ET with a 1-hour daily break (5pm–6pm ET). Crypto spot trades 24/7 — this difference creates CME gaps.
• Expiry: Last Friday of each month. Major quarterly expiry = March, June, September, December.

🕳️ What is a CME Gap? — The Most Misunderstood Concept in Crypto
A CME Gap forms when Bitcoin's spot price moves significantly during CME's closed hours (Friday 5pm ET → Sunday 6pm ET), creating a "blank space" on the CME chart that spot has already passed through but CME has not yet traded.

🔴 How a Gap Down Forms (Weekend Sell-off)
1. CME closes Friday at 5pm ET. BTC spot = $100,000
2. Saturday: bad macro news. BTC spot drops to $94,000
3. Sunday 6pm: CME reopens. First CME trade = $94,000
4. Gap down on CME chart: $94k–$100k zone never traded on CME

This $6k gap now "exists" on the chart. CME price jumped over it without trading through it.
🟢 How a Gap Up Forms (Weekend Rally)
1. CME closes Friday at 5pm ET. BTC spot = $95,000
2. Weekend: ETF approval news. BTC spot rallies to $102,000
3. Sunday 6pm: CME reopens. First CME trade = $102,000
4. Gap up on CME chart: $95k–$102k never traded on CME

Gap up above current price. Traders watching for "fill" if price pulls back to that zone.
Why Do CME Gaps Fill? The Theory:
CME gaps don't "fill" due to magic. They fill because:
1. Institutional arbitrage: Large players run algorithmic strategies that price CME and spot off each other. When a gap exists, there's a known price level that institutions modeled off — they often set orders around it.
2. Mean reversion tendency: Markets often retrace impulsive weekend moves, which "coincidentally" fills the gap.
3. Self-fulfilling: So many traders watch CME gaps that their collective behavior creates the fill.

Reality check: Not all gaps fill immediately. Some gaps from 2020–2021 took 1–2 years to fill. Don't build a whole strategy around "CME gap must fill now" — use it as one factor among many.

💰 Cash & Carry — How Institutions Harvest CME Basis
When CME futures trade at a premium to spot (contango), institutional traders run a cash & carry trade:

Step 1: Buy BTC spot (or ETF) = go long underlying
Step 2: Short equivalent CME futures = lock in the premium as profit
Result: Delta-neutral (no price exposure), earns the basis spread as annualized yield

Cash & Carry Yield = CME Basis % Annualized − Financing Cost

When CME basis = 15% annualized and T-Bills = 5%, the cash & carry earns a risk-free 10% premium. Billions of dollars run this trade.

The unwind risk: When basis collapses (bearish sentiment, ETF outflows, macro risk-off), these cash & carry trades unwind simultaneously. The fund sells spot (or redeems ETF) and buys back CME shorts. This creates coordinated spot selling that accelerates price drops — especially dangerous in the $70–90k range where many of these positions were built.
⚡ What Are Perpetual Contracts? — Start From Zero
A perpetual contract (perp) is a derivative that lets you trade BTC/ETH/alts with leverage but has no expiry date. Unlike CME futures which expire monthly, perps run forever. They're the most popular crypto trading instrument by volume — over $30–50B traded daily across all exchanges.

How they stay pegged to spot: The funding rate mechanism. Every 8 hours, either longs pay shorts or shorts pay longs a small fee, pulling the perp price back toward spot. If the perp trades above spot, longs pay shorts (incentivizes selling). If below spot, shorts pay longs (incentivizes buying).
💡 Funding Rate — The Single Most Important Derivatives Signal
Funding Payment (every 8h) = Position Size × Funding Rate %

Example: You're long $100,000 BTC on Binance perp. Funding rate = +0.05%/8h.
You pay: $100,000 × 0.05% = $50 every 8 hours = $150/day = $4,500/month just to hold the position. That's 4.5% monthly cost. Funding must be built into your trade plan.

💥 Liquidation Heatmap — The Price Magnet
A liquidation happens when a leveraged trader's margin balance falls below the maintenance margin requirement — their position is force-closed automatically.

If you go long $100k BTC at 10x leverage with $10k margin, and BTC drops 10%, your $10k is gone and your position is liquidated — creating a forced market-sell order that accelerates the drop further.

🔴 Long Liquidation Zone (Below Current Price)
Traders who bought with leverage at higher prices. If price drops to their liquidation level → their positions are force-sold → creates sell cascade.

Effect: Price "wicks" aggressively down to the cluster, triggers all those liquidations, then smart money buys the dip and price reverses sharply upward. The wick = liquidity grab.
🟢 Short Liquidation Zone (Above Current Price)
Traders who shorted with leverage at lower prices. If price rises to their liquidation level → their positions are force-bought back → creates buy cascade.

Effect: Price "squeezes" aggressively upward through resistance, triggering short liquidations, amplifying the move. This is a "short squeeze."

How to use: CoinGlass Liquidation Heatmap — set to 7-day view for significant clusters. Darker/more intense = more liquidations sitting there. Before entering any trade, check: is there a massive cluster $3–5% from current price? If yes — that cluster is likely to be hit before price reverses. Don't place your stop-loss inside a liquidation cluster. The market makers know where these are.
⚖️ Inverse vs Linear Perpetuals — Critical Difference
📐 Linear (USDT-Margined) — Recommended
Margin deposited in USDT (stablecoins). P&L settled in USDT.

Pros: Simple. Loss capped at your USDT deposit. No "double jeopardy."

Cons: Need USDT on the exchange.

Example: Long $10k USDT on BTC-USDT perp. BTC drops 10%. Lose $1,000 USDT. Simple.
🔄 Inverse (Coin-Margined) — Advanced Only
Margin deposited in BTC. P&L settled in BTC.

Danger: If you're long BTC and BTC price falls — your margin (in BTC) is also worth less. Double exposure. A 20% price drop feels like a 40% hit to your actual USD value.

Who uses it: Miners hedging native BTC income — they earn BTC, margin in BTC, hedge naturally.
🎯 What is an Options Contract? — Complete Foundation
An option gives you the right, but NOT the obligation, to buy or sell an asset at a specific price (the strike price) before or on a specific date (the expiry).

You pay a premium upfront to own this right. That premium is your maximum possible loss. Unlike futures, you cannot be liquidated — you can only lose what you paid for the option.

Simple analogy: You pay $500 for the right to buy a house for $800,000 anytime in the next 3 months. If the house goes to $1,000,000, you exercise your right and profit $200,000. If the house price falls to $600,000, you let the option expire and lose only the $500 premium. That is a call option.

🌊 Gamma Squeeze — The Amplification Engine
A gamma squeeze is when options market makers are forced to buy the underlying asset as price rises through call strike levels, which in turn pushes price even higher, forcing more buying. It creates a self-reinforcing loop.

How it works step by step:
1. Large BTC call OI sits at $110,000 strike. Market makers sold these calls.
2. To stay delta-neutral (not directionally exposed), market makers buy spot BTC as a hedge.
3. As BTC price approaches $110k, delta of those calls rises rapidly (gamma effect).
4. Market makers must buy MORE spot BTC to maintain delta-neutral hedge.
5. Their buying pushes price higher → delta rises more → they buy more → price squeezes.

Signal to watch: Large call OI clustering above a round number ($100k, $110k, $120k) + price approaching from below + low funding rate (not yet overleveraged) = gamma squeeze setup. The more concentrated the call OI at one strike, the more violent the squeeze. Check: CoinGlass Options OI by Strike
📅 Options Expiry — When, What, and Why It Moves Markets
Options expire on specific dates — after which they either pay out (if in-the-money) or become worthless. The expiry event is one of the most consistent price-moving forces in crypto.

Expiry TypeWhenTypical SizeMarket Impact
Weekly (Deribit)Every Friday, 08:00 UTC$500M–$2B notionalSmall. Creates minor intraday volatility around the settlement time.
Monthly (Deribit + CME)Last Friday of each month$3B–$8B notionalModerate. Max pain effect more pronounced. Watch the 3 days before.
Quarterly (CME + Deribit)Last Friday of Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec$10B–$25B+ notionalHigh impact. Largest OI concentrations. "Expiry week" volatility well-documented.

What actually happens at expiry:
1. ITM options get exercised — holders collect their profit (in USD or BTC depending on settlement)
2. OTM options expire worthless — holders lose the premium paid
3. Market makers flatten positions — they bought/sold spot to hedge; now they unwind those hedges
4. Large OI "evaporates" — billions in open interest disappears from the market, removing the gamma exposure that was affecting spot price dynamics
5. Post-expiry: market is "free" — without the gamma pinning effect, directional moves can resume more freely
🎯 Max Pain — The Most Actionable Options Concept
Max Pain = the strike price at which the TOTAL payout to all option holders (both calls and puts) is minimized — and therefore the pain to option BUYERS is maximized.

In other words: it's the price where the most option contracts expire worthless.

Why does price gravitate toward max pain?
Options market makers (banks, prop firms) are typically net short options — they sold calls and puts to clients. When options expire worthless, market makers keep the premium. So they have a direct financial incentive to push (or allow) price toward max pain before expiry. They do this via delta hedging — as their short options approach ITM status, they trade spot to push price away from those strikes.

Max Pain = Strike where (Σ Call Losses + Σ Put Losses) is MAXIMUM for option buyers
